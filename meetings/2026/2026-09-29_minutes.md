### WebMCP-Specific Threats and General Agent Threats

* Julia Pagnucco: Some entries describe the security properties of the wider agent ecosystem. I proposed moving those into another category or discussion. My reference to non-normative material came from the column suggesting specification additions or changes.

* Victor Huang: Agentic browsing exists with or without WebMCP. Carrying context between sites is one example. Structured tools may narrow some attack paths compared with screenshots or broad DOM interaction, but they do not make prompt injection or cross-site context transfer unique to WebMCP. We should document WebMCP-specific variants and possible mitigations, while making clear which problems WebMCP did not introduce.

* Joe Andrieu: A threat existing elsewhere does not remove the need to address the concern here. “Not us” and “not just us” are different answers. We should say when WebMCP does not have a particular threat and when it shares a problem that needs work across the ecosystem. I generally expect threat models to be non-normative.

* Julia Pagnucco: I am comfortable distinguishing those cases. I am less sure that detailed guidance for securing agents or browsers belongs in the web-platform specification. We have separate guidance for agent and site developers.

* Simone Onofri: The threat-class column can distinguish WebMCP-specific threats from general agentic browsing. Responses within the specification should link to its normative requirements; other responses may be guidance for agents or developers. We could record the concerns first and then decide where they belong, potentially in related threat models.

* Tom Jones: My concern is that some agentic browser functionality may never reach SING through a specification review. If we do not record these threats here, where will we address them?

* The location and scope of general agent guidance remain open. The discussion did not decide to remove the shared threats or to make them all WebMCP requirements.

### Access to User Data and Extension Permissions

* Tom Jones: By the user's “corpus,” I mean the data available to them, including through their logged-in sessions. I want the model to account for the agent's access to that data. Pointing to another access path does not resolve my concern about WebMCP.

* Julia Pagnucco: A browser or extension agent can already access logged-in session information through ordinary interaction with the page. I see that as an agent-level issue rather than something introduced by the WebMCP surface.

* Victor Huang: WebMCP gives site developers a way to expose selected operations for agents instead of relying on the human interface. That could help limit what agents need to access, although I cannot predict how agents will choose to use it.

* Joe Andrieu: A user may install an extension expecting WebMCP access without realizing they have granted broader DOM permissions. There may be an opportunity for a WebMCP-only permission. That would need work on the extension permission model; I am raising it as a possible response.

* Simone Onofri: We may need to discuss that with the people working on Web Extensions.
